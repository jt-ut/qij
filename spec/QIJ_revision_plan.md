**Terminology.** Vector-quantization usage throughout: a quantizer has prototypes; each prototype's Voronoi cell partitions the space; the data points in a Voronoi cell are that prototype's receptive field, and its mass is the receptive field's share of the data. The method has two quantizers: the **first-stage quantizer** on the data, and, for each estimator output, a **second-stage quantizer** on the learned influence values. "Estimator evaluation" is one run of the estimator on a weighted dataset. In prose, docstrings and reports, "fit" is used for neither the estimator nor the curve, and "cell" appears only in "Voronoi cell" and "influence cell". The identifiers this plan pins (`CellSet`, `cells.py`, `cell_weights`, `M_pilot`) are exempt; concise established names in code are preferred to circumlocution. The glossary in `QIJ_writeup_notes.md` is the reference.

**Persistence labels.** Every item below is tagged **[production]** (runs on any user's dataset, no study needed) or **[study]** (run once on the paper's eight estimands to fix constants and support claims). A user's run consists only of the [production] items: two declared inputs, derived rules, one pass, a report. No pilot studies, no per-dataset validation, no runtime checks that branch.

## 0. What the method is, and what is established

**Reviewer question.** How does QIJ work when the influence function ψ has no closed form?

**Why the existing derivative-free route falls short.** Finite differences along the masses of data-space receptive fields give only the between term S_btw. The within term, which the analytic route obtained from the formula for ψ, is 54–80% of the variance on the multivariate t and Fundamental Plane estimands, because ψ varies inside data-space receptive fields. Averages over receptive fields do not determine spreads inside them.

**The construction.** Two quantizers.
- **Prototype stage (first-stage quantizer, data space).** Fit a VQ to the data. Evaluate the estimator on the prototypes, weighted by their receptive-field masses, and difference it along each prototype's mass. These *coreset influences* are the contamination derivatives of the estimator at the quantized distribution, not the full-data influence at the prototype; they become close as the codebook grows. Pass a smooth curve through them: the *learned influence* ψ̃(x), available at every data point. Its level structure determines the second-stage quantizer; its numerical values also determine the automatic second-stage prototype count and, after one scale factor fixed from the measurements, the prediction S_survey,scaled and (wave 2) the explicitly model-based calibrated-field quantities. The measured tier depends on the curve only through the receptive fields it defines.
- **Full-data stage (second-stage quantizer, influence values).** For each estimand coordinate, a one-dimensional VQ of the learned influence values of the data points. Its Voronoi cells are intervals of the learned-influence axis; the receptive field of each second-stage prototype is the set of data points whose learned influence falls in its interval (these receptive fields are the *influence cells*). Within such a receptive field the true influence is nearly constant, so the between term captures nearly all of the variance. Measure the receptive-field averages with two full-data estimator evaluations per receptive field around one shared base evaluation: the existing QIJ machinery on new receptive fields.
- **Calibrated field (model-based addition, wave 2).** Re-solve the learned influence with the constraint that its average over each second-stage receptive field equals the measured average; read the small remainder, the covariance and the skewness off that field.

**What the evidence establishes** (probe, S = 50, N = 1000, analytic influence used only to evaluate): receptive fields built from the learned influence resolve about 99% of the oracle influence variance on the three smooth estimands tested, against 20–47% for data-space receptive fields.

| Estimand | data-space quantizer | learned-influence quantizer | ceiling (quantizer of true ψ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVT ν, ambient, M = 40 | 0.465 | 0.990 (M_pilot 300, kernel) | 0.997 |
| FP a, M = 50 | 0.339 | 0.990 (M_pilot 500, quadratic curve) | 0.991 |
| FP scatter, M = 50 | 0.199 | 0.990 (M_pilot 500, quadratic curve) | 0.989 |
| Pareto α, M = 50 | 0.996 | (already resolved) | — |

The Fundamental Plane figures come from the quadratic curve at 500 prototypes; the kernel curve at 100 prototypes explained 0.975 (a) and 0.960 (scatter). See §3.1 step 1 for the consequence.

Also established by the MVT fix (§5.4): on 100 centroids the estimator's value at the quantized distribution is far from the full-data value (ν̂ ≈ 240 against 5), while the probe's ordering of coreset influences still agreed with the truth at rank correlation ≈ 1. The prototype-stage value θ_Q = T(F_Q) estimates the functional at the quantized distribution, not at the empirical distribution; it is never reported as an estimate of the target parameter. Rank correlation alone does not validate the production use of the curve, because the curve's values enter the prototype count and the predicted tier; V0.5 therefore also reports slope, normalized RMSE and the second-moment ratio.

**What the evidence does not yet establish.** That the calibrated field recovers the remaining within variance, the third moment, or the covariance to any stated accuracy; that the rules for M_pilot and M reproduce the probe; the behaviour on the two tail-probability estimands. These are what the [study] gates test.

**Provisional headline (to be strengthened only after V0.5–V5 pass).** In a 50-draw probe, learned-influence receptive fields resolved about 99% of the oracle influence variance for MVT ν, FP a and FP scatter, so the measured between term is nearly the whole variance on these estimands; the calibrated field and the automatic rules remain to be validated.

## 1. Notation

- X: data, N × d. Z = `vq_data(X)`: the same N points in the first-stage quantizer's coordinates (N × d_z). W: first-stage prototype matrix (M_used × d_z) in Z coordinates; W_X: the prototypes in X coordinates (via the inverse transform, §3.5).
- p_j: first-stage receptive-field masses. θ_Q = T(W_X, M_used·p): the estimator's value at the quantized distribution. θ̂ = T(X, 1): the full-data value, evaluated once. ψ_Q(w_j): coreset influence at prototype j. ψ̃(x): learned influence. Influence cell k: the receptive field of second-stage prototype k (data points whose ψ̃ falls in Voronoi interval k). n_k, p_k = n_k/N: its size and mass. U_k: measured average of the true influence over receptive field k. c_k: measured second difference along receptive field k's mass. ψ̂: calibrated field.
- κ_c: the Panter–Dite constant of the learned influence values of coordinate c (§3.2).
- Scales: S_tot = (1/N)Σψ_i², S_btw = Σ_k p_k U_k², S_win = Σ_k p_k Var_k(ψ); Var(θ̂) = S_tot/N. All equations on the S scale unless they carry /N. Existing `V_cell`, `V_within` are S/N. Fix the paper's eq. (Vcell)/(identity) to one scale.
- Estimand coordinate: one entry of a vector-valued estimand (FP: a, b, c, scatter). Estimator evaluation: one call T(X′, ω) on a weighted point set.

## 2. User-declared inputs and disclosures [production]

Two declared inputs; everything else is derived.

- **η, the estimator's relative accuracy.** The size of the irreproducible part of one estimator evaluation, relative to the output's scale. It sets the difference step h = (3η)^{1/3} (§3.6) and nothing else. It is a declared order of magnitude, not a measured quantity. The step rule balances truncation (∝ h²) against noise (∝ η/h) under the assumption that the derivatives of T along a mass direction are of the output's own scale; at the rule's step the difference error is about η^{2/3}. Under that balancing model, and only as a scaling heuristic, a declaration wrong by a factor of 100 raises the error by a factor of about 5 (η declared too small) or 22 (declared too large); V4 measures the plateau on the study's estimators instead of relying on this. Rules for declaring it:
  - Closed forms with no numerically sensitive step (weighted means, the Pareto shape, the tail probabilities): machine precision, 2.2e-16.
  - Closed forms through a numerically sensitive step (the Fundamental Plane's eigendecomposition): larger; 1e-12 for the study.
  - Iterative solvers with a stopping tolerance on the parameter: that tolerance divided by the parameter's scale. (MVT ν is solved in log ν with tolerance 1e-12, which is already relative.)
  - Iterative solvers whose tolerance is on the objective or the gradient, or whose tolerance is not exposed: near a smooth optimum the parameter is resolved to about the square root of the objective's resolution, so declare the square root of the objective tolerance; if nothing is exposed, declare the square root of machine precision, 1.5e-8, which is where a double-precision iterative solver typically stops.
  - Vector estimators: one η, because one evaluation returns all coordinates and they share the step. Declare it for the least accurate coordinate, each coordinate's accuracy being relative to that coordinate's own scale (for FP: the slopes, the intercept and the scatter each on its own scale). V4 reports the difference error per coordinate.
  - Outputs at or near zero: "relative" is relative to the scale of the quantity the estimator computes, not to its value. An output that is a small difference of larger quantities declares η relative to the larger quantities.
  - **Valid range (documented, not enforced):** η > 0 and h = (3η)^{1/3} ≤ ½ (i.e. η ≤ 1/24), so that the one-sided rule's step +2h keeps every other weight non-negative. The user's declaration is the user's responsibility; the code does not check it.
- **ε_rf, the target receptive-field share** (`eps_rf`), default 0.01. The target share of the learned influence's variance left inside the second-stage receptive fields. Sets the second-stage prototype count M (§3.2) and, through the cost balance, the first-stage count M_pilot (§3.1). It is a target, not a guaranteed maximum: the count comes from an asymptotic formula for an optimal quantizer of a continuous density, evaluated with a kernel density estimate and realized by a locally optimal k-means; V3c measures the achieved share. Reading: if a share s of the variance is left inside the receptive fields and not counted, the interval is too narrow by about s/2; the default corresponds to about 0.5% in width.

There is no minimum receptive-field size and no parameter for one (§3.2 step 4 gives the reason).

**Disclosure for the paper.** (i) The estimator accepts an arbitrary weighted point set (ω ≥ 0, Σω = number of rows) and is well defined on coarse weighted data in the data's range; failure there is the estimator's defect. (ii) Non-smoothness: an estimator that is not differentiable in the weights (quantiles, hard thresholds, model selection inside T) is out of scope, as for every influence-based method. An estimator that is differentiable in the weights but whose influence is discontinuous in x (tail probabilities) is in scope for measurement; the learned influence is expected to smooth the jump and the curve's error there is reported. (iii) The coreset influences belong to the quantized distribution; θ_Q is the functional at that distribution and is not reported as an estimate of the target. Centroid quantization attenuates tails; the first-stage count grows with N so that the tail beyond the prototype hull shrinks (§3.1). The relative discrepancy ‖θ_Q − θ̂‖ / ‖θ̂‖ (per coordinate; NaN where θ̂ = 0) is reported as a diagnostic with no branch; a large value shows that the quantized distribution is far from the empirical one and does not by itself invalidate the ordering (the MVT case: ν̂_Q ≈ 240 against 5 with rank correlation ≈ 1). (iv) η is declared by the user and is the user's responsibility; the method does not check it at run time.

## 3. Algorithm

### 3.1 Prototype stage [production]

1. **Codebook.** `VQFitter` on Z via `QIJPartition.fit`, seeded (the only seed in the pipeline). Centroids. **The requested count is set by a cost balance between the two stages** (revision 3.3; derivation below, recorded for the paper in `QIJ_writeup_notes.md`):
   ```
   M_pilot = ⌈ √( (1 + 2 q M_ref) · N / 2 ) ⌉,    M_ref = ⌈ √(κ_ref / ε_rf) ⌉,  κ_ref = 2.7,    floor 20, cap N/2
   ```
   with N the number of data points, q the number of estimator outputs, ε_rf the declared target share (§2) and M_ref the second-stage count that ε_rf implies for normally distributed influence values (17 at the default). At N = 1000 and ε_rf = 0.01: 133 for a one-output estimator (√17500 = 132.3, rounded up; Pareto, MVT), 262 for the four-output Fundamental Plane.

   *Derivation.* Cost is counted in rows processed (§4). The full-data stage measures each of q second-stage quantizers with two evaluations per receptive field on all N rows around one shared base: (1 + 2 Σ_c M_c)·N ≈ (1 + 2 q M_c)·N rows. The prototype stage evaluates the estimator on M rows, twice per prototype plus once: (1 + 2M)·M ≈ 2M² rows. The rule gives the survey the same budget as the measurement it serves: 2M² = (1 + 2 q M_c)·N. M_c is not known before the run (it depends on the influence values through κ, §3.2), so the rule uses the count the Panter–Dite formula gives for normal values, κ_ref = 2.7; heavy-tailed influences get a somewhat larger count in the run and step-like ones a smaller one, but the rule needs only a representative measurement cost. The method's total cost is then known before running: about twice the measurement, (1 + 2 q M_ref)·N rows.

   *Why a cost rule and not an accuracy rule.* The wave-1 grid (§15) shows the curve's loss against the ideal receptive fields falling as 1/M² on the Fundamental Plane, where interpolation inside the hull limits the curve, and as 1/M for Pareto, where the tail beyond the prototype hull does; the ten-dimensional multivariate t needs fewer prototypes than the three-dimensional Fundamental Plane. The regime and its constant differ by estimator and are not predictable from anything a user knows before running (revision 2's d_z/ε² rule assumed the count scales with dimension and was wrong on every estimand). The budget is the one quantity known in advance. The grid then shows, as a finding and not a derivation, that at the budget-balanced count every smooth coordinate reproduces the ideal receptive fields to within 0.01 with margin (Pareto 0.0034 at 132; MVT under 0.001 at 132; FP 0.0009–0.0024 at 262). Two limits, stated in the paper: the count depends on q, a consequence of the budget statement, not an accuracy argument; and no a priori rule can guarantee accuracy on an unseen estimator, so the reported diagnostics (beyond-hull share, agreement (a), scale a_c) are what tell a user the survey was fine enough. The count grows with N because the tail beyond the hull is what the extra prototypes cover.

   N ≥ 40 is assumed (the bounds are then consistent and `QIJPartition.fit`'s two-BMU recall is valid); the CADJ metadata that class builds is not used. **Empty receptive fields are a normal outcome of vector quantization:** `QIJPartition.fit` drops prototypes whose receptive field is empty, so the number of prototypes actually used, M_used ≤ M_pilot, is what enters every later formula and count; both numbers are reported. Masses p_j from counts.
2. **Coreset influences.** θ_Q = T(W_X, ω = M_used·p). For each prototype, weights ω(t) = M_used·[(1−t)p + t e_j] (the generic constructor of §3.6 with base weights M_used·p and member set {j}), step h = (3η)^{1/3}, with the one difference rule of §3.6 (central where the downward step keeps every weight non-negative, one-sided second-order otherwise); then mass-center: ψ_Q ← ψ_Q − Σ_j p_j ψ_Q(w_j). Cost 1 + 2·M_used evaluations on M_used rows. No second differences are used from this stage.
3. **Learned influence.** Kernel regression with an affine mean and centers at the prototypes, per estimand coordinate: ψ̃(x) = h(x)ᵀβ + Σ_j α_j k(x, w_j), with h(x) = (1, x) in PCA-whitened Z coordinates (d_z + 1 basis functions) and k an RBF. It is written as Gaussian-process regression with explicit basis functions under a vague prior on β (Rasmussen & Williams 2006, §2.7) and prototype j's value trusted in proportion to its mass: ψ_Q(w_j) = h(w_j)ᵀβ + f(w_j) + e_j, f ~ GP(0, s² k_ℓ), e_j ~ N(0, s²λ / (M_used p_j)). Without the affine part this is the mass-weighted kernel ridge of revision 2; the affine part is added in revision 3.2 because a zero-mean RBF posterior reverts to zero beyond the outermost prototype, and on Pareto data (heavy-tailed, influence unbounded in log x) the most influential data points lie beyond the prototype hull and were assigned learned values near zero (§14, item 1). With the affine mean the curve continues the fitted trend beyond the hull, which preserves the ordering of monotone tails; the share of data beyond the hull is reported (§3.4). The basis is the constant alone when M_used ≤ d_z + 2. **The width ℓ and the strength λ are estimated by maximum marginal likelihood** (s² profiled out in closed form), not chosen from lists; details and the degenerate paths in §3.5. (The leave-one-prototype-out error e_shape of revisions 2–3.2 is removed in revision 3.3: wave 1 showed it 10²–10⁴ below the realized error, because it sees only the prototypes and the curve's failures were beyond them; §15.) Evaluate ψ̃ at all N points, streamed; `predict` returns the raw kernel values. Record the data-mean offset m_c = (1/N)Σ_i ψ̃_c(x_i) and **S_survey** = (1/N)Σ_i (ψ̃_c(x_i) − m_c)², the raw curve's second moment. The raw values are used for quantization (centering does not change the quantizer); α and β are never altered by centering; the offset is stored so that wave 2 can reproduce the exact field. S_survey is a diagnostic, not a prediction: the coreset influences carry the scale of the estimator at the quantized distribution, which can differ from the full-data scale by a large factor (MVT ν at 250 prototypes: θ_Q ≈ 22 against θ̂ ≈ 5, slope of coreset against true influence ≈ 12.6, S_survey ≈ 100 × S_btw). The prediction is made after the measurement supplies one scale factor (§3.2 step 5b).

### 3.2 Full-data stage [production]

4. **Second-stage quantizer**, per estimand coordinate c. The count is given by a formula, not searched. For an optimal one-dimensional quantizer with M prototypes, the share of the variance left inside the receptive fields is approximately κ/M² (Panter & Dite 1951; the one-dimensional case of Zador), with
   ```
   κ_c = (∫ f_c(v)^{1/3} dv)³ / (12 · Var(ψ̃_c))
   ```
   where f_c is the density of the learned influence values ψ̃_c(x_i) (κ = 1 for evenly spread values, ≈ 2.7 for normal values, larger for heavy tails, well below 1 for values concentrated at a few levels such as a smoothed step). Setting the target share to ε_rf:
   ```
   M_c = min( max(2, ⌈ √(κ_c / ε_rf) ⌉),  D_c ),   D_c = number of distinct values of ψ̃_c
   ```
   so the cap wins over the floor: a coordinate with one distinct value gets M_c = 1. κ_c is computed once from the learned influence values (§3.5, with the degenerate paths). Then 1-D k-means with M_c prototypes on ψ̃_c(x_i), quantile-initialized on the distinct values (deterministic; §5.5 pins ties, duplicates and empties during iteration). **Empty receptive fields are dropped** and the count actually measured, M_used,c ≤ M_c, is what enters the cost and the report; both are reported. **A single receptive field is measured trivially:** perturbing the whole data's mass changes no weight, so U = 0, c = 0, no evaluations, S_btw = 0. **There is no minimum receptive-field size and no merging.** Small receptive fields, including single extreme points that heavy-tailed influences put on their own, are measured as accurately as large ones (numerical check, Pareto shape, N = 1000, central rule at the rule's step: relative error 1.0e-6 for one point, 4.9e-7 for ten, 1.0e-7 for a hundred), and the only feasibility obstacle, a downward step that would make a weight negative, is removed by the difference rule of §3.6. The achieved share of Var(ψ̃_c) inside the receptive fields is recorded (reported, never used to change M_c). High-rate theory is least reliable for a smoothed or nearly discrete influence (the tail probabilities); there the count is still applied as written and the achieved share is the diagnostic. **No sharing of quantizers across coordinates**: every coordinate gets its own second-stage quantizer (FP: four). A joint quantizer of all coordinates' influence values was ruled out earlier: for the Fundamental Plane it is no better than the data-space quantizer, so it would understate each coordinate's variance. Every evaluation returns all coordinates, so the data needed to design a sharing rule later are collected for free.
5. **Measured receptive-field averages and second differences.** The full-data base value θ̂ = T(X, 1) is evaluated **once by the driver** and passed to every second-stage measurement. On each second-stage quantizer, differences along each receptive field's mass on the full data at h = (3η)^{1/3}, by the rule of §3.6, centered: U_k for all q coordinates; c_k the second difference. Coordinate c's measured quantities come from coordinate c's own quantizer: S_btw,c = Σ_k p_k U_{k,c}², bias_btw,c = (1/(2N)) Σ_k p_k c_{k,c}; the off-coordinate columns of U and c are kept as study data and never mixed into a report. Record r_c (the pre-centering residual Σ_k p_k U_{k,c} before centering; reported, no flag). Cost 2·M_used,c full evaluations per quantizer plus the one shared base: 1 + 2·Σ_c M_used,c in total.

5b. **One scale factor from the measurements** (wave 1, no evaluations). For coordinate c, ū_k = (1/n_k)Σ_{i∈k}(ψ̃_c(x_i) − m_c) are the centered curve's averages over its own receptive fields. The scale factor is the mass-weighted least-squares slope through the origin, a_c = Σ_k p_k U_{k,c} ū_k / Σ_k p_k ū_k² (NaN if the denominator is 0). The **scaled curve** is a_c(ψ̃_c − m_c); its second moment **S_survey,scaled** = a_c² S_survey is the PREDICTED tier's number; the **survey agreement ratio** is S_btw,c / S_survey,scaled; and **agreement (a)**, the weighted squared correlation (Σ_k p_k U_{k,c} ū_k)² / (Σ_k p_k U_{k,c}² · Σ_k p_k ū_k²), which is scale-free, moves from wave 2 into wave 1. One factor, fixed by measured numbers, is the smallest calibration that makes the curve's scale the full-data scale; the constrained solve of wave 2 then starts from the scaled curve and adjusts levels only.

### 3.3 Calibration [production, wave 2]

**Revision 3.5 (§19) replaces the kernel-space constrained solve of steps 6–7 below by a re-mapping of the learned influence on its own axis. Steps 6–7 are kept for the record; §19 is the specification.**

6. **Constrained solve.** The scaled curve of §3.2 step 5b (coefficients a_c·α, a_c·β, offset a_c·m_c) re-solved with M equality constraints, average over receptive field k = U_k, **with the prototype values treated as noisy at the level the measurements show them to be wrong** (revision 3.4, §17): the calibration's noise variance is the mass-weighted mean squared discrepancy σ_n² = Σ_k p_k (U_k − a_c ū_k)², a measured number, so its strength is λ_cal = σ_n² / (a_c² s²) in place of the curve's λ. The curve's λ, at its floor, made the field interpolate the coreset values exactly while also meeting the measured averages, and where the two disagree the field oscillated between prototypes (Pareto S_win_hat/S_win 11.9, MVT 24,000 in the production pass); with λ_cal the field relaxes smoothly toward the measured averages, and its within term can err only toward the measured bound. The within-receptive-field spread of the result is on the measured scale because of the factor a_c, not because of the constraints (the constraints pin averages and cannot rescale spreads; §14, item 2) (constraint matrix C_kj = (1/n_k)Σ_{i∈k} k(x_i, w_j), one pass). Solved by a rank-robust method (§5.5: least-squares solve of the KKT system giving the minimum-norm solution when C is rank-deficient). No condition-number branch, no penalty version, no flag; the constraint residual ‖Cα − U‖ / ‖U‖ is reported. The result is exactly calibrated only when that residual is zero; when C is rank-deficient and U lies outside its column space, the least-squares solve compromises between the objective and the constraints and the identity S_tot_hat = S_btw + S_win_hat no longer holds by construction. **Conventions (decided, revision 3.3, §16):** U is not projected; with M ≈ 14–19 constraints on M_used + m ≥ 130 unknowns the constraints are exactly satisfiable whenever C has full row rank, which is the generic case, and the minimum-norm least-squares solve covers the rest. S_win_hat = (1/N)Σ_i (ψ̂_i − Ū_{k(i)})² with Ū_k the calibrated field's own receptive-field averages, so S_tot_hat = Σ_k p_k Ū_k² + S_win_hat holds for the field by construction; the report shows U_k and Ū_k side by side and the residual is their difference. The curve's λ floor stays for the curve; the calibration uses λ_cal above; the conditioning problem is avoided by solving in transformed coefficients (§5.5).
7. **Two agreement numbers, reported.** (a) Weighted squared correlation between U_k and the curve's receptive-field averages ū_k (wave 1, §3.2 step 5b); at M ≤ 3 the relative discrepancy Σ_k p_k(U_k − a_c ū_k)²/S_btw instead. (b) Constraint effect: |S_field − S_survey,scaled| / S_survey,scaled, S_field = (1/N)Σψ̂_i². Both test the prototype stage against the measurements; neither tests within-receptive-field accuracy, which is unobservable at Level 3 (stated in the paper). No threshold, no flag, nothing withheld.

### 3.4 Report [production] — three tiers, labelled

```
MEASURED      S_btw = Σ_k p_k U_k²          first-order lower bound up to difference error (r_c reported)
              bias_btw = (1/(2N)) Σ_k p_k c_k   between-receptive-field curvature term (see note); an estimate, not a bound
PREDICTED     S_survey,scaled = a_c² S_survey   the curve's prediction of the total after one scale factor fixed from the measured averages
MODEL-BASED   S_win_hat = (1/N)Σ(ψ̂_i − U_{k(i)})²;  S_tot_hat = S_btw + S_win_hat  (= (1/N)Σψ̂_i² when the constraint residual is zero)
```

*Note on bias_btw.* It is the Hessian contraction of the estimator over multinomial fluctuations in the span of the receptive-field masses: a between-receptive-field curvature term. It omits second-order directions inside receptive fields, and influence alignment (a first-order property) does not imply that the omitted curvature is small. V6 compares it with the known O(1/N) bias; using it to centre the interval is conditional on V6 passing, and if V6 fails the interval stays centred at θ̂ and bias_btw remains a reported diagnostic. The manuscript must not present it as the full second-order bias.

### 3.5 Specified defaults and conventions [production]

- **Inverse transform.** `BaseDGP` gets `vq_data_transform(X) -> (Z, inverse)` with the identity as default, reshaping to (N, −1) so that one-dimensional data (Pareto) become a column as `study.py`'s fallback did. W_X = inverse(W). Identity for FP and Pareto (FP prototypes are passed to the estimator in raw X coordinates; the estimator applies its own frozen population standardization internally, stated in the docstring). For MVT, the ambient branch of `vq_data` is implemented as `vq_data_transform(X)[0]` so the two cannot diverge, and the transform is the per-coordinate mean/sd of the sample with the returned inverse closure capturing those same values; the radial branch of `vq_data` is **unchanged** (the existing radial study depends on it) and `vq_data_transform` raises `NotImplementedError` for the radial input. `IMFDGP` keeps its own `vq_data`. (Revision 3.1 said "`vq_data` routed through the transform" and "radial raises" without distinguishing the two methods; the coordinator's reading above is the ruling.)
- **Weights.** Every evaluation passes weights summing to the number of rows; both stages differentiate along e_j − p, the convention of U_j; §3.6 gives the one constructor.
- **Whitening.** PCA whitening from the full Z, one pass; prototypes and data mapped identically; standardization when d_z = 1.
- **Width and strength of the learned influence curve (maximum likelihood).** One width ℓ (isotropic in whitened coordinates) and one strength λ per estimand coordinate. Minimize the negative log marginal likelihood with s² profiled out (§5.5), over (log ℓ, log λ), with `scipy.optimize.minimize` (L-BFGS-B, **analytic gradient**, §5.5; revision 3.2 said numerical gradients, which cost three objective evaluations per iteration and made half the starts end in line-search failures on flat likelihoods). Bounds from the data: ℓ between the smallest non-zero inter-prototype distance and **ten times** the largest inter-prototype distance (whitened; the factor ten because the largest-distance bound was hit on every FP coordinate in the trial runs, §14 item 4: a very wide RBF is the curve's way of being a low-order polynomial, and a bound that binds is not a bound from the data); λ between 1e-10 (numerical conditioning) and 1e2 (noise a hundred times the signal). Six starts, deterministic: ℓ at ½, 1 and 2 times the median inter-prototype distance, each with λ at 1e-3 and 1e-1, each start clipped into the bounds. A start whose optimizer run fails or returns a non-finite objective is discarded with its status recorded; the kept optimum is the lowest finite objective among all completed runs and all evaluated start points, ties broken by start order, so a result always exists. Report whether ℓ or λ ends within 1% (in log) of a bound, the optimizer status per start, and the wall time of the maximization (measured, not assumed negligible).
  - **Constant-response path.** If M_used < 3, or no positive inter-prototype distance exists, or the mass-weighted standard deviation of ψ_Q,c is ≤ 1e-10 × max(|θ_Q,c|, tiny) (tiny = 1e-300), the coordinate's curve is the constant ψ̄_c (zero after centering): no optimization; α = 0; ℓ, λ = NaN; s² = 0; at_bound = False; `predict` returns the constant; m_c = ψ̄_c; S_survey = 0; the second stage then finds one distinct value, M_c = 1, S_btw = 0 (§3.2), and the survey agreement ratio is NaN. The path taken is reported. This is the expected outcome for a tail probability whose threshold no centroid crosses.
- **Panter–Dite constant κ_c.** Density f_c of the N learned influence values by a Gaussian kernel density estimate with Silverman's bandwidth, bw = 0.9·min(sd, IQR/1.34)·N^{-1/5}; **if IQR = 0 but sd > 0, bw = 0.9·sd·N^{-1/5}**; if sd = 0 the coordinate is on the constant path and κ is not computed (κ = NaN, M_c = 1). f_c is evaluated on 512 equally spaced points spanning the values ± 3 bw; ∫f^{1/3} by the trapezoid rule on those points (underflow to zero in the tails is harmless; f^{1/3} is finite everywhere); Var(ψ̃_c) the plain variance of the values. κ is then finite and positive; the cap D_c keeps M_c ≤ N. (The 512 points are numerical quadrature, not a tuning choice.)
- **Centering.** ψ_Q mass-centered (step 2); U_k centered by the difference routine; ψ̂ is then centered automatically; ψ̃ raw for quantization, data-centered by the stored offset m_c for S_survey.
- **η for the study's estimators** (in config, not code): Pareto shape, tail probabilities: machine precision. FP: 1e-12. MVT ν: the solver's tolerance in log ν, 1e-12.
- **ε_rf for the study:** the default, 0.01. First-stage counts by the rule of §3.1: 132 (Pareto, MVT), 262 (FP).
- **Oracle at non-data points [study]:** the estimand's `influence_at(X_eval, X_fit, omega_fit)` (§5.4), called on the unwrapped estimator, never through the counter. `psi(X, θ)` is not used at non-data points.
- **Randomness.** Only the codebook seed.

### 3.6 Weights and the difference rule (both stages) [production]

**One weight constructor.** Given base row weights ω⁰ (summing to the number of rows R) and a member set K of rows with mass p = Σ_{i∈K} ω⁰_i / R, the weights at signed step t along e_K − p are
```
ω_i(t) = (1 − t) ω⁰_i + t · ω⁰_i · 1{i ∈ K} / p          Σ_i ω_i(t) = R for every t
```
On the full data ω⁰ = 1 and K = influence cell k, which gives ω_i = (1−t) + t·N/n_k on the cell and (1−t) off it. On the prototypes ω⁰ = M_used·p and K = {j}, which gives ω_j = M_used[(1−t)p_j + t] and ω_i = (1−t)M_used p_i elsewhere. (Revision 3's instruction to reuse the label/count helper with unit counts on the prototype stage was wrong: it would replace the prototype masses by 1/M_used.) The constructor performs no checks.

**The difference rule.** For a receptive field (or first-stage prototype) with mass p and step h = (3η)^{1/3}, write T(t) for the estimator at ω(t) (t = 0 is the base value, already evaluated once and shared):
```
central     (when the downward step keeps every weight non-negative, i.e. p ≥ h/(1+h)):
            U = [T(+h) − T(−h)] / (2h)                      c = [T(+h) − 2T̂ + T(−h)] / h²
one-sided   (otherwise):
            U = [−3T̂ + 4T(+h) − T(+2h)] / (2h)              c = [T̂ − 2T(+h) + T(+2h)] / h²
```
Both use two evaluations beyond T̂. U has O(h²) error under both rules. The one-sided c has O(h) error against the central rule's O(h²); the number of one-sided receptive fields is reported so that the bias term's accuracy can be read. The one-sided case arises only when a receptive field holds fewer than N·h/(1+h) points: never at N = 1000 with the study's η (N·h ≈ 0.14), and below about 15 points at N = 10⁵ with η = 1e-12. The rule and the constructor are written once (`differences.py`) and used by both stages.

**1-D k-means.** Lloyd's iteration on the sorted 1-D array in `cells.py`; do not route it through VQFitter. Initialization: the M_c quantiles at levels (k + ½)/M_c of the **distinct** sorted values (distinct, so the initial prototypes are distinct; M_c ≤ D_c guarantees this). Assignment: intervals bounded by the midpoints between consecutive prototypes, closed on the right, so a value exactly at a midpoint goes to the lower prototype (`np.searchsorted(midpoints, values, side='left')`). (Revision 3.1 wrote "left-closed", which is the opposite interval; the stated outcome, lower prototype, was always the intent and is what is implemented.) Update: each prototype to the mean of its receptive field; a prototype whose receptive field is empty after an assignment step is dropped immediately and the remaining prototypes relabelled, so no mean is ever undefined. Convergence: assignment unchanged, or a cap of 100 iterations. Final labels are contiguous and ordered by prototype value.

**Kernel regression specifics (with the affine mean).** RBF k(x, w) = exp(−‖x − w‖²/(2ℓ²)) in whitened coordinates; basis matrix Hb (M_used × m) with rows h(w_j) = (1, w_j), m = d_z + 1 (m = 1, constant only, when M_used ≤ d_z + 2). With D = diag(1/(M_used p_j)) and A = K + λD (Rasmussen & Williams 2006, §2.7.1, vague-prior limit, eqs. 2.41–2.45):
```
β  = (Hbᵀ A⁻¹ Hb)⁻¹ Hbᵀ A⁻¹ ψ_Q            α = A⁻¹ (ψ_Q − Hb β)
profiled s² = ψ_Qᵀ Cm ψ_Q / (M_used − m),   Cm = A⁻¹ − A⁻¹ Hb (Hbᵀ A⁻¹ Hb)⁻¹ Hbᵀ A⁻¹
NLL (up to constants) = ((M_used − m)/2)·log s² + ½·log det A + ½·log det(Hbᵀ A⁻¹ Hb)
prediction  ψ̃(x) = h(x)ᵀβ + Σ_j α_j k(x, w_j)
```
via one Cholesky factorization of A per objective evaluation (Hbᵀ A⁻¹ Hb is m × m and is factorized too). **Analytic gradient** (restricted-likelihood form): with P = Cm, for θ ∈ {log ℓ, log λ},
```
∂NLL/∂θ = ½ tr(P ∂A/∂θ) − ((M_used − m)/2) · (ψ_Qᵀ P (∂A/∂θ) P ψ_Q) / (ψ_Qᵀ P ψ_Q)
∂A/∂log λ = λ D          ∂A/∂log ℓ = K ∘ D2 / ℓ²   (D2 the matrix of squared whitened distances)
```
The log det(Hbᵀ A⁻¹ Hb) term is what turns A⁻¹ into P in the trace, so no separate term appears. The gradient formula was verified once by the coordinator (agreement 1e-10 with finite differences on well-conditioned problems, and on real draws at a step above the objective's roundoff); no gate checks it. P need not be formed: with A⁻¹ from the Cholesky once per evaluation, tr(P ∂A) = tr(A⁻¹ ∂A) − tr(G⁻¹ (A⁻¹Hb)ᵀ ∂A (A⁻¹Hb)) with G = Hbᵀ A⁻¹ Hb, the correction costing O(M² m). Implementation efficiencies that leave results unchanged to rounding (no finite checks inside the factorizations, K cached per ℓ, in-place kernel build, no redundant solves) are allowed without plan changes. **Jitter:** if the factorization of A fails, add jitter 1e-10·trace(K)/M_used to the diagonal (escalating tenfold per failure, at most three escalations) and **restart the whole maximization of that coordinate from its six starts** with the new jitter, so that every candidate is compared on the same A; the final jitter stays in A for α, β, the hat matrix and the leave-one-out, and the jitter and the number of restarts are recorded in `ShapeModel`. (No leave-one-out is computed; revision 3.3.) Constrained solve (wave 2), on the scaled curve (§3.3), with the mass weights Wm = M_used·P. In coefficient space the normal matrix K Wm K + λK has condition number about cond(K)², beyond double precision at the λ floor (cond(A) reached 1e13 in wave 1). Therefore solve in transformed coefficients: with K = L Lᵀ (Cholesky, using the curve's jitter) put γ = Lᵀ α, so Kα = Lγ and αᵀKα = γᵀγ, and the objective is (a ψ_Q − Lγ − Hbβ)ᵀ Wm (a ψ_Q − Lγ − Hbβ) + λ_cal γᵀγ in (γ, β), with λ_cal = σ_n²/(a² s²) from §3.3 (revision 3.4), whose normal matrix [[LᵀWmL + λI, LᵀWmHb],[HbᵀWmL, HbᵀWmHb]] has condition about cond(K), within precision. Constraints: C_γ γ + C_β β = U with C_γ = C_α L⁻ᵀ (C_α,kj = (1/n_k)Σ_{i∈k} k(x_i, w_j), one pass over the data; C_β,k = average of h over receptive field k; L⁻ᵀ by triangular solves). KKT system [[N, Cᵀ],[C, 0]] solved by `scipy.linalg.lstsq`; α = L⁻ᵀγ only to evaluate the field at the data, ψ̂(x) = h(x)ᵀβ + Σ_j α_j k(x, w_j), streamed. Report ‖Cγ,β − U‖/‖U‖. With λ_cal in place of λ the unconstrained minimizer is no longer the curve itself but a smoother version of it, by design.

Gates run draws sequentially by default with a `--n-jobs` flag using joblib as the study does; BLAS thread counts are the environment's (`OMP_NUM_THREADS`), not set by the scripts.

**Gate aggregation rules.** The unit is (estimator object, coordinate, draw). For each coordinate the table reports the median, the 5th and 95th percentiles and the worst value over the draws that evaluated, plus the number of failed draws. A criterion stated as a bound ("within 0.01", "< 1e-3", "≥ 0.95") is applied to the **median over draws**, per coordinate; a coordinate passes if its median meets the bound and at most 5% of its draws failed to evaluate; "on smooth estimands" means every coordinate except the two tail probabilities must pass; the tail probabilities are reported, never gated. A "stable plateau" (V4) means the median relative error of S_btw against the analytic between term is below 1e-3 at h/2, h and 2h. The summary states pass/fail per coordinate by these rules and never by a different aggregation.

## 19. Revision 3.5: calibration as a re-mapping on the influence axis (16 September 2026, 23:10)

**Why.** The production rerun (§18) shows the kernel-space calibration right on Pareto and FP and wrong on MVT ν (S_win_hat/S_win 654). The cause is structural: on MVT the learned influence is a monotone but nonlinear distortion of the true one (the estimator sits at ν ≈ 22 on the quantized distribution against 5 on the data), the cells are right (99% of ideal), and a kernel field in ten dimensions with 133 centres cannot represent a nonlinear re-mapping of its own values without oscillating between prototypes; exact constraints then force the oscillation whatever the prototype term's weight. The fix removes the mechanism: the calibrated field is a function of the learned influence alone, so its spread inside an influence cell is bounded by the cell's own range of learned values times the local slope of the map.

**Specification (per estimand coordinate c; no estimator evaluations).**
Inputs: the raw learned values ψ̃_i (N), the influence-cell labels (M cells, ordered by increasing mean ψ̃, all non-empty), the centered measured averages U_k, the masses p_k.
1. Knots t_k = mean of ψ̃ over cell k, k = 1..M (strictly increasing, since the cells are ordered disjoint intervals of the ψ̃ axis).
2. The map m is continuous and piecewise linear through (t_k, y_k), extended linearly beyond t_1 and t_M by its end segments. In hat-function form m(v) = Σ_j y_j B_j(v), with B_j the usual hat functions on the knots and the end hats extended linearly outside [t_1, t_M] (so B_1(v) = 1 + (t_1 − v)/(t_2 − t_1) and B_2(v) = (v − t_1)/(t_2 − t_1) for v < t_1, and symmetrically above t_M). m is linear in y for every v.
3. The knot values solve the M × M system A y = U with A_kj = (1/n_k) Σ_{i∈cell k} B_j(ψ̃_i). A is tridiagonal (cell k's values lie between its neighbours' knots) and diagonally dominant; solve with `numpy.linalg.solve` (fall back to `lstsq` only if singular, and report). Report the residual ‖Ay − U‖/‖U‖ (expected at rounding).
4. The calibrated field: ψ̂_i = m(ψ̃_i). Its cell averages equal U_k by construction, and Σ_k p_k U_k = 0, so ψ̂ is mass-centered up to rounding.
5. Outputs, unchanged in meaning: S_win_hat = (1/N)Σ_i (ψ̂_i − U_{k(i)})², S_tot_hat = S_btw + S_win_hat = (1/N)Σ_i ψ̂_i², Var_hat (diagonal), a_hat from ψ̂'s third moment, the `accel` interval from the field; the `measured` interval unchanged and still the headline.
6. Diagnostics replacing agreement (b): the segment slopes s_k = (y_{k+1} − y_k)/(t_{k+1} − t_k), k = 1..M−1; report min slope, max slope, the number of negative slopes, and the map's linearity, the mass-weighted R² of y_k regressed on t_k (1 for a pure scale; the MVT case will be visibly below 1). The scale factor a_c and agreement (a) stay as the prototype-stage diagnostics and the PREDICTED tier (S_survey,scaled) stays.
7. Degenerate cases: M_used = 1 or the constant path → ψ̂ ≡ 0 (as D3); M = 2 → one segment through two knots, extrapolated both ways.
8. Removed: `shape.constrained`, λ_cal, σ_n², the KKT solve and the Cholesky-transformed coefficients (§3.3 steps 6–7, §5.5 "Constrained solve"); the field no longer lives in the kernel basis. `report.py` builds ψ̂ from `ShapeModel.predict` and the `CellSet`.

**Bound stated for the paper.** Inside cell k, Var_k(ψ̂) ≤ (max slope on the cell)² · Var_k(ψ̃), so S_win_hat ≤ (max_k s_k)² · Σ_k p_k Var_k(ψ̃) = (max_k s_k)² · (achieved within share) · Var(ψ̃); with the achieved share at 1–2.4% and a slope variation of even 3×, S_win_hat stays below about a fifth of S_btw. The kernel calibration had no such bound.

**Expected effect and risk.** MVT ν: S_win_hat/S_win from 654 to order one. Pareto and FP: the map is close to a straight line (a_c 0.93–1.00), so S_win_hat should land within 10–20% of the current values (0.83–0.97 of S_win), possibly slightly lower, since the kernel re-solve added a little wiggle of its own. Shared and unchanged limit: the curve's within-cell error that does not follow its own levels (Pareto's beyond-hull points, about 1% of the data) is invisible to both constructions.

**Run:** one production pass at the same settings; V5, V6, V6b, V8 and T1 regenerated from the final records after the pass completes; then figures. No checks added (§5.3 stays the only one).

## 20. Revision 4 (17 September 2026, morning): measurement by adaptive refinement — specification for review, not yet dispatched

**Why.** The production pass of 16 September (23:21) validated the measured tier on the six smooth coordinates but showed four defects the authors will not publish: the tail probabilities measure only 56–88% of the variance (coverage 0.83–0.92); the learned curve rings (Fig. F9), because a Gaussian-process interpolant with one coefficient per prototype at a noise floor of 1e-10 is ill-conditioned; the curve's likelihood maximization is 14 of the Fundamental Plane's 15 s of wall time, more than its bootstrap; and the calibration stage is a corrective patch that moves smooth outputs by 1–2% and does nothing structural. Revision 4 replaces the curve and folds the calibration into the measurement, which becomes an adaptive refinement of the influence cells with the data-space VQ's connectivity (CADJ) supplying both the curve's neighbourhood and the uncertainty that guides refinement. The count rule (§3.1), the second-stage count (§3.2) and the tier structure of the report are unchanged. Terminology is on hold (authors, 17 Sept) until the results are publishable; then "survey", "coreset", "pilot", "prototype stage", "full-data stage" become the data-space VQ (stage 1), the influence-space VQ (stage 2), the prototype influence, the learned influence and the measurement.

### 20.1 Stage 1: data-space VQ and the learned influence
1. Codebook and count as in §3.1 (M_1 requested, M_1,used after dropping empty receptive fields). `QIJPartition.fit` already returns the CADJ and CONN graphs; they are now used.
2. **Prototype influences by forward differences.** ω(t) = M_1[(1−t)p + t e_j], step h_f = 2√η (the forward rule's balanced step; error about 2√η relative, 3e-6 at η = 1e-12, far below the 1% the level structure needs); ψ_Q(w_j) = [T(ω(h_f)) − θ_Q]/h_f, mass-centered. Cost 1 + M_1 evaluations on M_1 rows (was 1 + 2M_1). Upward steps only; always feasible.
3. **Learned influence: local affine fit on the CADJ neighbourhood.** For prototype b, the neighbourhood N(b) is b together with its CONN neighbours; while |N(b)| < d_z + 2, add the CONN neighbours of its members (second order, third order, …). The CADJ graph is not connected in general, so this extension can exhaust b's component before reaching d_z + 2; when it does, fill N(b) to d_z + 2 with the nearest remaining prototypes in whitened Euclidean distance (the fixed-count fallback, used only where the graph runs out). Fit ψ_Q ≈ α_b + β_bᵀ(w − w_b) over N(b) by **unweighted** least squares in whitened coordinates (minimum-norm solution if rank-deficient). Not by mass: every prototype influence is an equally exact derivative, and mass weighting would let dense neighbours override a sparse tail prototype's own value inside its own receptive field. Not by tricube distance weights either (a first draft used them with r_max the farthest member, which gives that member weight zero, so a minimal neighbourhood of d_z + 2 prototypes is interpolated exactly and s_b ≡ 0; on the Pareto chain graph every neighbourhood is minimal, so no uncertainty split could ever fire). The neighbourhood supplies the locality; every member keeps full weight; a minimal neighbourhood leaves one residual degree of freedom. The learned influence of every data point i in receptive field b is ψ̃_i = α_b + β_bᵀ(x_i − w_b): one fit per prototype, applied to its own receptive field; affine, so it cannot ring, and it extrapolates as the local trend. Record s_b, the root-mean-square residual of fit b over N(b): near zero where the influence is locally affine, large where it jumps inside the neighbourhood.
4. **Point uncertainty is the local fit's misfit.** u_i = s_{b(i)}. (A first draft used half the influence difference across the point's CADJ edge; that is the error of nearest-prototype assignment, not of affine interpolation, and on a smooth influence in ten dimensions it exceeds the within-cell variation by an order of magnitude, which would have driven the refinement of §20.2 toward single points. The edge information enters through the neighbourhood of item 3 instead.) Points whose neighbourhood fit is clean have u ≈ 0; the one blind spot is a point beyond a threshold whose whole neighbourhood lies inside, stated in the paper.
5. Nothing else: no width, no strength, no likelihood, no jitter, no condition number, no leave-one-out, no beyond-hull share (superseded by s_b).

### 20.2 Stage 2: measurement by refinement, per estimand coordinate
6. **Initial cells.** 1-D k-means on ψ̃ with M from §3.2 (Panter–Dite, unchanged). Measure every initial cell with the three-point one-sided stencil of §3.6 at h = (3η)^{1/3}: U_k (second order) and c_k. Cost 2M full evaluations plus the shared base. bias_btw = (1/2N)Σ_k p_k c_k from these cells (unchanged; it matches the exact Pareto bias to 2% and is kept as implemented bias correction).
7. **Expected gain of a leaf.** A leaf with n_k > 1 carries a proposed split and an expected gain. If Var_k(ψ̃) ≥ mean_k u², the split is by level (2-means on ψ̃ inside the cell) and the expected gain is g_k = a²·[p_a ū_a² + p_b ū_b² − p_k ū_k²], the between-children variance of ψ̃ on the measured scale. Otherwise the split is by uncertainty: points with u above the cell's median u against the rest; if that side is empty (u is constant within each first-stage receptive field, so ties are common), points with u ≥ median against u < median; if that is empty too (all u equal), by level; g_k = a²·p_k·mean_k u², the within variance the misfit could be hiding. (Points carry equal mass, so the median is the plain median.) A cell with n_k > 1 but a single distinct ψ̃ value and u = 0 has no split and is closed with g = 0; with u > 0 the uncertainty branch applies. A single-point leaf has no within variance, cannot be split, and carries g = 0.
8. **Scale of the prediction:** a² = S_btw / Σ_k p_k ū_k² over the current leaves, with ū_k the mean of ψ̃ over leaf k: the ratio of the measured to the predicted between term. It replaces the scale factor a_c and the map of §19 and is recomputed after every split.
9. **Refinement loop, stopped by measured gains.** Let τ = ε_rf·S_btw/|leaves| (ε_rf the declared target share, default 0.01), each leaf's share of the tolerated remainder, recomputed as S_btw and the leaf count change; a split is judged against the τ in force when it was chosen. Repeat: take the open leaf with the largest g_k; if g_k < τ, stop. Split it; measure the smaller child with **one forward evaluation** at h_f, U_small = [T(ω_small(h_f)) − θ̂]/h_f; derive the larger child by mass balance, U_large = (p U_parent − p_small U_small)/p_large (its error is the small child's scaled by p_small/p_large ≤ 1); record which leaves were measured and which derived. The measured gain is Δ = p_a U_a² + p_b U_b² − p_k U_k² ≥ 0 (exactly, by the identity; up to difference error in practice). Update S_btw += Δ and a². If Δ < τ the lineage has converged at the tolerance scale: both children are closed (g = 0). Otherwise both children get their own proposed split and expected gain. (A first draft stopped on the predicted remainder; that cannot work, because a predicted within made of point uncertainties is conserved under splitting. Measured gains are what adaptive quadrature stops on.) **Cost guard:** the refinement may spend at most as many evaluations as the data-space stage did, 1 + M_1,used; if the guard binds it is reported. Residual risk, stated: a split that happens to balance a straddling cell yields a small Δ and closes the lineage early; for a step this occurs only when the jump lies at the end of the cell's ramp, where the within variance is small, and the model-based remainder of item 10 still counts the leaf.
10. **Outputs.** MEASURED: S_btw = Σ_leaves p_k U_k² (a lower bound, rising monotonically through the refinement, exact up to difference error); bias_btw. MODEL-BASED: Ŝ_win = a²·Σ_{leaves with n_k > 1} p_k [Var_k(ψ̃) + mean_k u²] (the predicted remainder on the measured scale; includes the misfit term, so it is conservative; single-point leaves contribute nothing); Ŝ_tot = S_btw + Ŝ_win; the field ψ̂_i = U_{k(i)} + a(ψ̃_i − ū_{k(i)}) for the third moment and a_hat; Var_hat diagonal. INTERVAL: the measured interval (headline) and the acceleration-corrected interval from Ŝ_tot, as now. There is no PREDICTED tier and no separate calibration: the prediction's accuracy is the ratio of measured to predicted split gains, reported. DIAGNOSTICS: number of leaves; numbers of level and uncertainty splits; a²; the gain ratio over the refinement splits (Σ measured Δ divided by Σ expected g of the splits made); r_c; the largest remaining g_k; number of derived leaves; whether the cost guard bound.
11. **Degenerate cases.** Constant prototype influences: ψ̃ constant, u = 0, one cell, no evaluations, S_btw = 0, Ŝ_win = 0, reported. M_used = 1: same. A vector estimator: each coordinate has its own tree; every evaluation returns all coordinates and the off-coordinate values are kept as data.

### 20.3 Cost
Stage 1: (1 + M_1)·M_1 rows. Stage 2 per coordinate: 1 base (shared) + 2M + (L − M) full evaluations for L leaves. Predictions at N = 1000: one-output estimand M_1 = 133, M ≈ 15, L ≈ 20: 134·133/N ≈ 18 + 1 + 30 + 5 ≈ 54 normalized rows (was 66.5); FP M_1 = 262, four trees of L ≈ 20: 69 + 1 + 4·(38 + 5) ≈ 242 (was 277); tail probabilities L ≈ 50: about 20 + 1 + 4 + 48 ≈ 73. Wall time: the estimator evaluations plus milliseconds of local fits and k-means; the 14–19 s of likelihood maximization is gone. The count rule of §3.1 balances 2M_1² against (1 + 2qM_ref)N; with stage 1 now costing M_1² rows the balanced count would be √2 larger; **the count rule is kept as it is** (its accuracy is established by the grid at these counts) and the saving is taken as cost.

### 20.4 What the paper can then say
Two stages, two declared inputs, one procedure: a data-space VQ measures the estimator's derivatives at its prototypes cheaply and, through its connectivity graph, supplies a local affine influence model with a per-point uncertainty; an influence-space VQ is then measured on the full data and refined, one evaluation per split, where the model varies or is unsure, until the model's predicted remainder is below the declared share, with the model's accuracy measured on the splits themselves. The between term of the leaves is the result. Bias correction and skewness correction come from the same evaluations.

### 20.5 Expectations and risks (stated before running)
- Smooth coordinates: S_btw/S_tot at or above the current 0.98–0.99; totals within 1%; cost down 15–20%; wall time down by an order of magnitude on FP.
- Pareto tail probability: the uncertainty split isolates the straddling-edge points and level splits localize the jump; expected S_btw/S_tot above 0.97 at about 50 leaves.
- MVT tail probability: the sub-cell study (15 Sept) recovered 80% of the variance with second-BMU sectors on data-space cells at M = 40; with 133 prototypes and the same information used as an uncertainty coordinate, expected above 0.9; the blind spot of item 4 sets the ceiling and is reported.
- Risk: the local affine fits are discontinuous across receptive-field boundaries (one fit per prototype); the level quantizer sees small steps at the boundaries, which the refinement can split and measure, so the effect is cost, not error.
- Pitfalls found on review before dispatch (17 Sept) and corrected above: the edge-difference uncertainty (would have over-refined smooth estimands); prediction-based stopping (conserved under splitting, so unattainable); mass weighting in the local fit (would suppress tail prototypes in their own receptive fields); single-point leaves needing zero within variance; tricube weights with r_max at the farthest member (zero weight there, s_b ≡ 0 on minimal neighbourhoods; replaced by unweighted least squares, ruling of 17 Sept).

### 21.1 Stage 1: data-space VQ and the learned influence
1. Codebook, count rule, `QIJPartition.fit` with CONN and second-BMU: as §20.1 items 1–2 (forward differences at h_f = 2√η, 1 + M_1 evaluations).
2. **Curve: Gaussian process with affine mean and Matérn 3/2 kernel.** ψ_Q(w_j) = h(w_j)ᵀβ + f(w_j) + e_j, h(x) = (1, x) in whitened coordinates (constant only when M_1,used ≤ d_z + 2), f ~ GP(0, s²k_ℓ) with k_ℓ(r) = (1 + √3 r/ℓ)·exp(−√3 r/ℓ), r the whitened distance, and **unweighted noise** e_j ~ N(0, s²λ) (D = I; every prototype influence is an equally exact derivative, §20.1; the mass-weighted noise of revision 3 let dense neighbours override sparse tail prototypes). Posterior mean and the vague-prior treatment of β as in §5.5 (Rasmussen & Williams §2.7.1), with D = I. The Gaussian kernel is replaced because its smoothness produces the ringing seen in F9 across sparse gaps and the 1e13 condition numbers; the Matérn 3/2 kernel is the standard default, well conditioned, with little overshoot between samples, so a step in the prototype influences becomes a nearly monotone ramp that the level refinement can localize.
3. **Hyperparameters by maximum marginal likelihood, strength profiled per width** (the coordinator's option C, §14 item 11): for each ℓ, one symmetric eigendecomposition of K_ℓ with the affine basis projected out; the profiled likelihood in log λ is then O(M) per value and is minimized by a bounded scalar search on [1e-10, 1e2]. ## 24. Additional module (17 September 2026): a joint (X, I) second-stage quantizer with a model-derived local metric — comparison study, not a replacement

**Purpose.** Test whether a single joint quantizer of data space and the influence axis, under a local metric computed from the stage-1 model, matches or beats the I-VQ + refinement of §21 at the same evaluation count. The existing pipeline is untouched; this is a new module and a new study script. If the results are good the authors may adopt it; the paper cites it either way as the regression analogue of localized relevance learning (GRLVQ, Hammer & Villmann 2002; GMLVQ and its localized form, Schneider, Biehl & Hammer 2009; the outer-product-of-gradients / MAVE estimators, Xia, Tong, Li & Zhu 2002).

Names in this section: *receptive field* = a data-VQ cell (stage 1); *bin* = an I-VQ cell; *joint cell* = a cell of the quantizer specified here.

### 24.1 The local metric, derived
We want cells with small within-cell variance of the true influence ψ. Write ψ = ψ̂₀ + e, with ψ̂₀ the stage-1 posterior mean and e its error. Within a cell, Var(ψ) ≈ Var(ψ̂₀) + Var(e). The first term is what the I-VQ minimizes; to first order in the cell's extent, (Δψ̂₀)² ≈ (∇ψ̂₀·Δx)², the squared distance along the local gradient. The second term has two sources, each with a scale in x:
- **Resolution error.** Between prototypes the model interpolates; its error is of order ½ h² ‖∇²ψ̂₀‖ with h the local prototype spacing, second order for a smooth influence and of order |∇ψ̂₀|·h at a jump (where the model's ramp has curvature ~ |∇ψ̂₀|/h). Two points a distance D apart share this error only if D ≲ h, so its within-cell variance grows like (½h‖∇²ψ̂₀‖)²·|Δx|²/h² = ¼h²‖∇²ψ̂₀‖²·|Δx|².
- **Posterior uncertainty.** Where prototypes are sparse (tails, beyond the hull) the posterior standard deviation u is large; the posterior error is correlated over the kernel width ℓ, so its within-cell variance grows like (u/ℓ)²·|Δx|².
Adding the three terms gives a squared distance between two points that is Euclidean under a local metric:
```
d²(x, x') = (Δx)ᵀ Λ(x) (Δx),     Λ(x) = ∇ψ̂₀(x) ∇ψ̂₀(x)ᵀ  +  c(x)²·I,     c(x)² = ¼ h_b(x)² ‖∇²ψ̂₀(x)‖_F²  +  u(x)²/ℓ²
```
The first term is the influence axis (the level-set quantizer's metric). The isotropic term is negligible where the model is smooth and confident, so there the cells are level-set slabs, and it takes over where the model bends over one prototype spacing (a jump) or is unsure (sparse regions), where the cells become compact in x. Everything in Λ is computed from the stage-1 model: ∇ψ̂₀ and ∇²ψ̂₀ are analytic (sums of Matérn-3/2 kernel derivatives plus the affine slope β; for k(r) = (1 + ar)e^{−ar}, a = √3/ℓ: ∇_x k = −a² e^{−ar}(x − w), and the Hessian is k''(r)nnᵀ + (k'(r)/r)(I − nnᵀ) with k'' = −a²(1 − ar)e^{−ar}, k'/r = −a² e^{−ar}, n = (x − w)/r, finite at r = 0), u is the posterior sd already computed, ℓ the kernel width, and h_b the median distance from the point's nearest prototype b to its CADJ neighbours. No learned quantities, no constants beyond the ¼ that comes from the ½ in the Taylor remainder.

### 24.2 The quantizer
A per-point metric cannot drive a plain k-means, so the quantizer uses per-cell metrics in the manner of localized GMLVQ, with the metrics computed rather than learned. Embed each point as (ψ̂₀_i, x_i) with x in whitened coordinates. Cell k has a centre (m_k, μ_k) and an isotropic weight c_k. Lloyd iteration:
- assignment: point i goes to the cell minimizing (ψ̂₀_i − m_k)² + c_k²·|x_i − μ_k|²;
- update: m_k, μ_k the means over the cell; c_k² the mean of c(x_i)² over the cell;
- initialize from the I-VQ bins at the same M (their means and c's); iterate to unchanged assignment or 100 iterations; drop empty cells and relabel. Deterministic.
(The first-order term ∇ψ̂₀∇ψ̂₀ᵀ is carried by the ψ̂₀ coordinate itself, since (Δψ̂₀)² ≈ (∇ψ̂₀·Δx)²; only the isotropic term needs the per-cell weight.)

Predicted remainder Ŝ_win = a²·Σ_k p_k [Var_k(ψ̂₀) + mean_k(u_res² + u²)] with u_res = ½h_b²‖∇²ψ̂₀‖_F; total, field, acceleration and both intervals as in §21. Diagnostics: per-cell share of the isotropic term c_k²·(cell x-radius)² against the ψ̂₀ variance, the cells' elongation (ratio of x-radius to ψ̂₀-range in metric units), and the oracle R² of the joint cells against the I-VQ bins at the same M.

The MVT tail's excess is the model's within-bin variation, not its uncertainty.** Item A was right as far as it went (v_k is the correct uncertainty term and stays) and wrong about the source: with V_btw/V_tot = 1.000 the bins are pure, so the true within-bin variance is zero, but ψ̂₀ is a smooth ramp and still varies inside those bins, so the Var_k(ψ̂₀) term of V̂_win is positive where the truth has nothing.   V̂_win = ρ² (1/N) Σ_k p_k [γ_k Var_k(ψ̂₀) + v_k],  γ_k = min(1, g/ĝ) of the last split in bin k's lineage (γ_k = 1 for a bin never split),

## 31. Revision 6 (19 September 2026): the finite-difference step is relative to the mass it moves; the within-bin term is scaled entirely by realized gain

**Finding (TACC production, package 60e8359, N = 2000 everywhere).** Seven coordinates clean (coverage 0.936–0.954, widths within 1%, V̂_tot/V_oracle 0.98–1.00). The IMF over-estimated its variance by about 46% on every output. Diagnosis, in two steps, both refuted or confirmed by measurement: (i) evaluation noise is not the cause: the IMF reproduces to 5e-7 against its declared η = 1e-6, and that noise contributes about 1e-8 of V. (ii) The step is: with η = 1e-6, δ = (3η)^{1/3} = 0.014 in absolute mass raises a 1% bin to 2.4% and nearly doubles a prototype's mass (M_X = 414, δ_f = 0.002); on the one estimator whose parameters respond nonlinearly to the tail the finite difference is a chord, not a derivative. At η = 1e-12 on the same draws V_btw/V_oracle went from 1.70, 10.0, 509, 1.00, 1.04 to 0.94–1.00 on all five outputs; the between term then sits at or just below the oracle, as a lower bound should. The centering residual is machine zero at both steps. The absolute-mass step has perturbed small bins proportionally far more than large ones for every estimator; only an estimator with a large η made it visible.

**Rule 1 (both stages).** The step is a fixed fraction of the mass being moved. For a bin (or prototype) K of mass p_K, the weights at relative step s are ω_i(s) = (1 − t) + t·1{i ∈ K}/p_K with t = s·p_K/(1 − p_K), so the mass of K becomes p_K(1 + s) and every other weight falls in proportion. The stencils are unchanged in form, taken at s = δ and 2δ with δ = (3η)^{1/3} (three-point, stage 2 initial bins) or at s = δ_f = 2√η (single forward step, stage 1 and refinement); U_K is the derivative with respect to t, i.e. the stencil's derivative with respect to s times (1 − p_K)/p_K. The balance of truncation against noise is the same derivation as before, now in the natural unit of a mass derivative, and the relative noise in each U_K is the same for every bin whatever its mass. Feasibility: every weight stays non-negative for any s < 1/… (the downward direction is never used). For the closed-form estimators the change is not measurable; for the IMF it is the fix.

**Rule 2 (the predicted within-bin term).** V̂_win = ρ² (1/N) Σ_k p_k γ_k [Var_k(ψ̂₀) + v_k], with γ_k scaling the whole bracket. At η = 1e-12 the IMF's derived outputs (γ_shape, γ_scale) had V_btw at 0.99 of the oracle and V̂_tot at 1.7–2.3: their inflation is the v_k term, the model's self-assessed uncertainty, kept while the refinement's realized gains (gain ratio 0.01) said the bins hold no variation the model predicts. γ_k is that measured evidence for the bin's lineage, and the model's uncertainty is part of the same prediction it discounts. Never-split bins keep γ_k = 1, and they have small v_k by construction (a large one would have been an expected gain above τ). Nothing changes where γ_k ≈ 1 (the smooth coordinates) or where γ_k clips to 1 (the MVT tail).

**Consequence for the paper's IMF claim.** The bimodality of p under resampling is real and stays the finding: a replicate that loses the rare massive stars converges to a second mode. On the method's side the earlier "factor of 509" was the chord crossing into that mode, not the method suffering the bimodality; with a proper derivative QIJ does not. The paper says so.

The cost products stand: Rule 1 changes the values of the finite differences, not how many are taken, so the FP cost sweep's counts, wall times and (its steps being a hundredfold smaller) its numbers are unchanged to rounding, the IMF cost sweep is used for counts and wall times only, and the timing pass is unaffected. ## 33. Revision 7 (19 September 2026): the adjacency split is triggered by prototype-influence disagreement, not by model uncertainty

**Why.** §32: at N = 2000 the MVT tail's adjacency splits stay at 16 per draw while the straddling mass grows, because the split-kind rule sends a bin to the adjacency branch only when its uncertainty σ exceeds its ψ̂₀ variation, and σ shrinks as prototypes get denser. The tool that resolves a straddle was gated by a quantity that vanishes with N.

**Rule.** Every bin with more than one point carries two candidate splits, each with an expected gain in variance units:
- level: two-means on the bin's ψ̂₀ values; ĝ_lvl = ρ²·(between-children variance of ψ̂₀ over the bin)/N, as before;
- adjacency: points whose second-nearest prototype carries a higher prototype influence than their nearest, against the rest (level split if one side is empty); ĝ_adj = ρ²·p_k·mean_{i∈k} (½ |I_{b(i)} − I_{b₂(i)}|)² / N, the variance the split would recover if the truth jumped by the edge difference inside the bin.
The refinement queue takes the largest expected gain over all candidates of all open bins; the stopping share τ and the closing of a lineage by realized gain are unchanged; σ no longer enters any proposal (it remains in v_k). On a smooth coordinate the edge difference measures a gradient, not a jump, so an adjacency split it proposes realizes little and its lineage closes after one evaluation: at most one extra evaluation per bin. On a discontinuous influence adjacency splits keep realizing gains and their count scales with the straddling mass.

**Expected:** MVT P_tail at N = 2000 from 0.913 toward 1.0 of the oracle; the seven clean coordinates and the IMF unchanged to rounding with slightly more refinement evaluations; Pareto P_tail unchanged. **Run:** the main study once, for one code; the cost products stand (evaluation counts change only by the extra refinement evaluations on the tails, which the main study's own counts report).

## 34. Revision 7, addition (19 September 2026): stored bin constituents and the second-order QIJ interval

**Ruled by the author:** the main study reruns at N = 2000 (a uniform N at the pool's size has no truth to compare to; 2000 keeps both methods' finite-sample behaviour visible, and the cost sweep shows each vanishing with N), with the tail fix of §33 and two additions.

**Stored constituents.** The `qij` product gains, per draw and output, the final bins' masses p_k, bin influences U_k and, for the initial bins, the second differences Δ²T_k (L values, at most a few kilobytes per draw). They are what the between-bin term, the bias term and the interval below are computed from, and they were missing from the products.

**The second-order QIJ interval, computed downstream, no estimator evaluations.** The estimator over the bin masses has the quadratic surrogate
  T(p + Δp) ≈ T̂ + Σ_k U_k Δp_k + ½ Σ_k Δ²T_k Δp_k²
(the diagonal of the Hessian in the bin masses, from the stencils; off-diagonals are not measured). The multinomial fluctuation of the masses is known, Cov(Δp) = [diag(p) − ppᵀ]/N. Draw Δp from the multinomial R = 10 000 times (bin counts ~ Multinomial(N, p), Δp = counts/N − p), evaluate the surrogate, and take the (1 − q)/2 and (1 + q)/2 quantiles of T̂ + (surrogate − T̂): the second-order QIJ interval h^{QIJ2}_q. Its mean over draws is T̂ + B̂ (the bias term is the mean of the quadratic term), its variance is V_btw plus the second-order between-bin term, and it carries the skewness of the quadratic term. Where the curvatures are small it coincides with the first-order interval; on the IMF it is the test of whether the between-bin curvature is what the linearization misses. For the initial bins Δ²T_k comes from their stencils; for bins created by refinement (measured with one forward step) the curvature is taken as the parent's, since a child's mass direction lies within its parent's.

**Reported for every coordinate beside h^{QIJ}_q:** coverage and width of h^{QIJ2}_q. **Decision rule for the paper, stated before the run:** if h^{QIJ2} brings the IMF's M* to nominal coverage (within one Monte-Carlo standard error of 0.95) and leaves the other twelve coordinates within one standard error of their first-order values, the paper presents QIJ to second order as the method's interval (the same evaluations giving the bias as the mean of the quadratic term and the interval from its distribution) and the first-order interval as the model-free reference; if it does not, the first-order interval stays and the IMF's gap is reported as the linearization's limit.

## 35. The second-order interval in closed form (19 September 2026)

The author's objection stands: the surrogate S = T̂ + uᵀΔp + ½ΔpᵀDΔp is a quadratic form in Δp whose distribution is known, and simulating it by multinomial draws is Monte Carlo on tractable arithmetic; it also reads, wrongly, as a bootstrap. **Rule:** the second-order interval is computed from the cumulants of S under the Gaussian approximation to the multinomial, Δp ~ N(0, Σ) with Σ = (diag(p) − ppᵀ)/N (justified at the bin occupancies in use, about N/L ≳ 30 points per bin), and a Cornish–Fisher expansion for the quantiles. For a Gaussian quadratic form with D = diag(Δ²T_k) and u = (U_k):
  κ₁ = ½ tr(DΣ)  (= B̂, the bias term, by construction)
  κ₂ = uᵀΣu + ½ tr((DΣ)²)  (uᵀΣu = V_btw exactly, since Σ_k p_k U_k = 0)
  κ₃ = 3 uᵀΣDΣu + tr((DΣ)³)
  κ₄ = 12 uᵀΣ(DΣ)²u + 3 tr((DΣ)⁴)
All are L × L matrix products; microseconds. The quantile at level α: z_α + (z_α² − 1)γ₁/6 + (z_α³ − 3z_α)γ₂/24 − (2z_α³ − 5z_α)γ₁²/36, with γ₁ = κ₃/κ₂^{3/2}, γ₂ = κ₄/κ₂², and h^{QIJ2}_q = T̂ + κ₁ + √κ₂ · quantile at the two tails of level q. **Validation, once:** against the R = 10 000 multinomial sampler on the main run's stored constituents, all thirteen coordinates, all seven levels, plus the two cumulant identities (κ₁ = B̂, uᵀΣu = V_btw) against the stored values. The §34 decision rule is applied to the closed-form construction. Bins created by refinement carry their parent's Δ²T (§34).

**Author's ruling, 19 September, later (supersedes the last clause above):** the sampler does not survive anywhere in the shipped interval, not as a default and not as a far-tail fallback for one level on one coordinate. QIJ's claim is that it does not resample; an interval that reaches for Monte Carlo, even over a surrogate, invites a conclusion the paper should never have to talk a reader out of. The multinomial sampler is a development-time validation reference only and is removed from the package once the closed form is validated. The decision tree is:
  1. Cornish–Fisher, fourth order, as above, if it agrees with the sampler at every coordinate and level.
  2. Otherwise exact numerical inversion, deterministic and seedless: factor Σ = SSᵀ, write the surrogate as ½yᵀAy + bᵀy with y ~ N(0, I), A = SᵀDS, b = Sᵀu; eigendecompose A; rotating and completing the square gives a weighted sum of independent noncentral χ² variables (weights the eigenvalues, noncentralities from the components of Vᵀb), plus a normal term and a constant on the zero-eigenvalue subspace; Imhof's method gives the distribution function by quadrature and the quantile by a root-find. L is at most low hundreds. Cornish–Fisher is an approximation to this, so where they disagree the inversion is believed.
  3. The sampler, never.
Whichever closed form survives is the construction the §34 rule is applied to; the rule is not applied to sampler-built numbers. A material disagreement between Cornish–Fisher and the inversion is reported to the planner before the rule is applied.

### 36.2 Rulings

1. **Refinement rule (replaces §33; rev 6's form with one substitution).** One proposal per bin with more than one point: a level split (two-means on ψ̂₀) if Var_k(ψ̂₀) > v_k, with ĝ = ρ²·(between-children variance of ψ̂₀ over the bin)/N; otherwise an adjacency split (points whose second-nearest prototype carries the higher prototype influence, against the rest; level if one side is empty) with ĝ = ρ² p_k v_k / N. v_k is the within-bin posterior variance already computed for V̂_win (v_k ≤ mean_k σ_i², the bin-common part of the posterior removed). Queue, τ, closing by realized gain, γ_k, cost guard: unchanged. Reason for v_k over σ²: v_k is the within-bin variance the model actually predicts, hence the gain a split can recover; σ² includes the bin-common uncertainty no split recovers, and under ruling 2 it grows 300× on the IMF, which would put rev 6's σ-priced rule at the stopping threshold on every IMF bin.
2. **The influence model's noise is declared, not fitted below the declared level.** Per coordinate c, n_c² = 2 η² θ_Q,c² · median_j(1/t_j²), t_j = step_parameter(δ_f, p_j). The noise-to-signal ratio λ_c is searched by REML as now but on [max(λ_floor,c, 1e-10), 1e2], where λ_floor,c solves λ·s_c²(λ) = n_c² with s_c²(λ) the profiled closed form (λ·s²(λ) = (1/(M−m)) Σ_i z_i² λ/(Λ_i + λ) is increasing in λ, so the root is unique; brentq on log λ, no iteration). For the closed-form estimators the floor is below 1e-10 and nothing changes (verified on MVT ν draw 0: unchanged to 1e-11). σ_i, v_k, ψ̂₀ follow from the same posterior.
3. **The box rule applies to every bounded estimator.** A ν fit at either end of its bracket [0.1, 1e6] returns NaN, with the tolerance 1e-4 of the box width **in the coordinate the search walks in** (log ν for the ν root-find; the three bounded parameters directly for the IMF). *(Corrected after the pre-flight: applied in ν itself the tolerance would be 100 and every fit below ν = 100.1 would read as a bound hit.)* Any other bounded estimator likewise (audit the FP and Pareto fitters; expected none).
4. **A collapsed stage 1 is a failed draw.** If the base evaluation on the prototypes fails, or the influence model takes its constant path for an output, that output's QIJ result is NaN (V_btw, V̂_tot, B̂, a_bca, interval), counted as failed. Zero-variance results are never produced.

The bootstrap's replicate-failure fraction stays as is.

### 36.8 The tail shortfall is the closing rule, not a limit (19 September 2026; per-split trace, scratchpad `tail_trace/`)

Three draws traced under the revision-8 build (reproduced to 1e-8 against the products): s = 0 (r = 1.000, L 72, 55 refinement evaluations, stopped with no open leaves), s = 431 (median, r = 0.911) and s = 301 (5th percentile, r = 0.748), both stopped by the queue test (largest ĝ < τ). On the two short draws **every final bin holding a tail point is closed**, and the true gain still available in those bins (from the two-valued oracle influence) **equals the shortfall V_oracle − V_btw to four figures** (4.60e-7 and 1.43e-6) and exceeds the stopping τ by 600× and 2700×. The impure closed bins are sibling pairs: a parent holding two tail points was split so that each child received one; the children's bin influences are equal, the realized gain is below τ (6e-10 against 1.2e-9, or 1e-21), and the closing rule of §3.6 ("if g < τ, close both children") closed both. Each such child has n_k = 2–17 with one tail point, v_k = 2e-3 to 1e-2, and an adjacency expected gain ρ² p_k v_k/N of 5–20 τ: the model marks the bins, and the closing rule prevents the queue from ever seeing them. At s = 0 the same situation did not arise. §36.1's "limit" and §36.7's "restores rev 6" are both withdrawn: the shortfall is a rule I wrote, and it has been in every revision since 5.

**Not yet ruled.** A realized gain near zero says the split was uninformative, not that the children are pure; only the model's within-bin uncertainty can tell those apart, and it is what the proposal already prices. Removing the closing rule (children always re-proposed; the queue test and the cost guard unchanged) recovers the tail on these draws. Its risk is cost: ĝ_adj/τ ≈ ρ² v_k/(N ε V_btw) does not fall with L, so a coordinate with large v_k anywhere would split to the guard (1 + M_𝒳 evaluations), which is the runaway the closing rule was written to stop. Whether any coordinate does so under the declared noise floor is an empirical question, to be answered on the laptop before any ruling: every coordinate, closing rule removed, Pareto/MVT 50 draws, FP 20, IMF 3, reporting L, evaluations, V_btw/V_oracle and V̂_win/V_btw against revision 8.

**Candidate, not ruled:** close a lineage after TWO consecutive splits below τ rather than one. Rationale: one near-zero realized gain cannot separate a pure bin from one whose needles were divided evenly (§36.8's sibling pairs); a child holding a single needle cannot divide it again, so its next split realizes a gain if impure and none if pure.
